What Is Supported Independent Living (SIL)? A Plain-English Guide

A plain-English guide to Supported Independent Living (SIL) under the NDIS: what it covers, how it differs from SDA, and how to get started in Western Sydney.

By the NavAura Care team · 15 November 2026

Key points
  • SIL is help with daily tasks in a shared or individual home, from drop-in to 24/7 support.
  • SIL pays for support, not the house. Rent and living costs are usually paid by the participant.
  • SDA is different: it is funding for specially designed housing for people with very high support needs.
  • Your SIL provider and your housing provider can be different organisations.

Supported Independent Living, usually called SIL, is one of the most asked-about NDIS supports. This guide explains what it is, what it covers and how it fits with housing.

What SIL is

SIL is help or supervision with daily tasks so you can live as independently as possible. It is usually provided in a shared home with other people with disability, but it can also be provided in your own home. Support can range from drop-in visits to 24/7 support, including overnight support.

What SIL can help with

  • Personal care such as showering, dressing and grooming
  • Cooking, cleaning, shopping and household tasks
  • Taking medication as prescribed, with prompting
  • Managing money and household bills
  • Building skills for greater independence
  • Overnight support, active or sleepover

What SIL does not pay for

SIL funds the support, not the house. Rent, groceries, utilities and other day-to-day living costs are usually paid by the participant, often from their own income such as the Disability Support Pension.

SIL and SDA are different

Specialist Disability Accommodation (SDA) is funding for specially designed homes for people with extreme functional impairment or very high support needs. Some people have both SIL and SDA in their plan, but many people receive SIL in ordinary rental housing. Your SIL provider and your housing provider do not have to be the same organisation, and you can choose each one.

How SIL gets into your plan

The NDIA decides whether SIL is reasonable and necessary based on your support needs and evidence such as functional assessments. A support coordinator can help you gather information, explore housing options and talk to SIL providers.
